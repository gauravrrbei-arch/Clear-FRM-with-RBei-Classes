FRM Part 1 and Part 2 Syllabus

FRM has two examination parts, each focusing on different aspects of risk management. Understanding the syllabus and topic weightings helps candidates organize their preparation according to the learning objectives published for their exam year.

FRM Part 1

FRM Part 1 develops the foundational knowledge needed to understand financial risk. The four main topic areas are Foundations of Risk Management, Quantitative Analysis, Financial Markets and Products, and Valuation and Risk Models. Candidates should pay particular attention to probability, statistics, derivatives, valuation techniques and risk measurement concepts.

Candidates can use the dedicated FRM Part 1 study planner to organize their learning schedule, set topic-wise goals and allocate time for practice and revision.

FRM Part 2

FRM Part 2 focuses on applying risk management principles to real-world financial scenarios. Its subject areas include market risk, credit risk, operational risk and resilience, liquidity and treasury risk, risk management in investment management, and current issues in financial markets. Candidates should combine conceptual learning with scenario-based questions and regular revision.

The FRM Part 2 study planner provides a dedicated starting point for organizing Part 2 preparation and tracking study progress. Candidates should confirm that their materials match the official syllabus for the examination year they intend to take.

For a broader topic-by-topic overview, visit the complete FRM syllabus guide, which provides additional information about the curriculum and examination structure.

FRM Exam Dates and Examination Fees

GARP generally offers FRM examinations in May, August and November. However, the exact dates, registration windows and available examination options can vary by year and examination part. Candidates should consult the official GARP website before selecting an exam window or making a payment.

For 2026, the published fee structure lists a one-time enrollment fee of USD 400 for eligible new candidates and an examination registration fee of USD 600 per part during early registration or USD 800 per part at the standard registration rate. These amounts should be verified against the applicable GARP fee schedule, as policies and pricing may change.

Candidates registering for both parts should account for the applicable enrollment and examination fees. Currency conversion, bank charges and taxes, where applicable, may affect the final amount paid. Coaching tuition is separate from GARP's official examination charges, so students should budget for both.

To avoid relying on outdated dates or prices, check the official GARP FRM program page for current registration deadlines, examination dates, eligibility requirements and fees. Candidates should also verify any restrictions that apply to their chosen examination window.

How to Build an Effective FRM Preparation Routine

An effective preparation routine combines structured learning with regular assessment. Start by reviewing the official syllabus, estimating the time available before the examination and identifying subjects that require additional practice. Set realistic weekly targets instead of trying to complete too many topics in a single session.

  1. Understand the syllabus: Review the official learning objectives and divide the curriculum into manageable sections.
  2. Study concepts thoroughly: Use lectures, notes and examples to understand the reasoning behind financial formulas and risk models.
  3. Practice consistently: Solve topic-wise questions and revisit concepts that cause repeated mistakes.
  4. Take mock examinations: Simulate examination conditions to improve timing, accuracy and confidence.
  5. Revise and evaluate: Track your progress, analyze mock-test results and adjust the study plan where necessary.

Candidates should avoid relying entirely on lectures or postponing revision until the final weeks. Active problem-solving and regular review make it easier to identify weaknesses early. The best study schedule is one that can be followed consistently and adjusted as preparation progresses.
